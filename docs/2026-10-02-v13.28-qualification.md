# Upstream v13.28.0 qualification against the fork's contracts (HAR-1103)

Date: 2026-10-02. Fork `local/stable` = `02bc9068`. Upstream tag v13.28.0 = `57c38303` (published 2026-09-26). Upstream `main` = `039c6160` (includes #4226 quota windows, #4331 hook stdout flush, neither released yet).

## Method

Our 13 contract test files from `local/stable` were copied, unchanged, onto two untouched upstream checkouts with a private `CLAUDE_MEM_DATA_DIR`, one Bun process per file. Control: upstream's own versions of the same ten shared files pass on v13.28.0 (284 pass, 0 fail), so a failure below is a difference between the fork's expectations and upstream, not a broken harness. Fork-only files: `claude-provider-stream-order`, `hook-output-drain`, `scripts/audit-local-install`. Nothing was installed; no worker was touched.

Also measured: `git merge-tree local/stable v13.28.0` conflicts in 26 files (6 `src/`, 5 `tests/`, 15 manifests and built bundles). The fork's CI now runs on `local/stable` and `candidate/**` (PR #2, all five jobs green on a hosted runner).

## What this means

- Upstream's next release should cover contracts 1 (mostly) and 7. Contracts 2, 3 and 5 are real gaps that the fork must keep carrying. Contract 4 is a product choice. Contract 6 is the expensive unknown.
- The patch layer therefore does not disappear with v13.28.x. A maintained candidate is still required, and its hard part is contract 6: either port `TurnGate` onto the pacer-based `ClaudeProvider`, or write probes that show upstream's re-queue and stall handling preserve work the way the fork does, and drop the fork's version.
- Retiring the fork is not available yet. The 2026-09-30 upkeep design's weekly qualification would have reported `blocked` on its first run for exactly the conflicts measured here.
